Technical & product review

Systematic Crypto Trend Engine

Status as of 9 October 2026 · Data: Binance, Jan 2020 – Sep 2026 · All results net of fees, spread, market impact and funding · Research software — not financial advice

1Executive summary

We built a research-grade engine that tests and runs systematic crypto trading strategies on Binance perpetual futures and spot, then publishes the resulting positions as signals. The core strategy is multi-horizon trend following with risk-based position sizing, which can go long or short. Everything is designed around one principle: a backtest only counts if it would have been achievable in real life. That means no peeking at future data, realistic trading costs, coins that later collapsed kept in the test, and a locked period of data the strategy was never tuned on.

Bottom line for reviewers. The engine delivers roughly BTC-like returns with about one-third of BTC's drawdown, and it made money in both rising and falling markets. The evidence is encouraging but not yet conclusive: on unseen data the 95% confidence interval of the Sharpe ratio still includes zero, and one overfitting test is a warning (Section 12). The product therefore launches as a transparent, public paper-trading track record before any paid signals.

2What the product is — and why

What we doWhy we do it
Trade a portfolio of liquid coins, not single “hot calls”Diversification across coins and horizons is the most reliable way to raise return per unit of risk.
Follow trends over 20, 60 and 120 daysTrend following is the most consistently documented edge in crypto in academic studies; averaging three horizons avoids betting on one “magic” lookback.

4Data

  • Source: Binance official historical archives. Hourly spot and USDT-M perpetual bars plus every funding payment, Jan 2020 – Sep 2026 (about 59,000 hours per coin).
  • Universe: 16 large coins: BTC, ETH, BNB, SOL, XRP, DOGE, ADA, AVAX, LINK, LTC, DOT, TRX, BCH, EOS, LUNA and FTT. Why include LUNA and FTT? Both collapsed. Testing only on coins that are still alive today (“survivorship bias”) makes any backtest look better than reality.
  • Integrity: checksum-verified downloads, zero duplicate or bad rows, maximum gap 5 hours, synthetic bars flagged and never traded.
  • Spreads: measured from Binance order-book archives where they exist; otherwise estimated with the Abdi–Ranaldo (2017) method, which deliberately overstates spreads to stay conservative.
  • In progress: a point-in-time universe that picks the most-traded coins each month from every perp ever listed, to remove the remaining selection bias.

5Strategies

5.1 Trend (core, live)

  1. For each coin, check whether the price is above or below where it was 20, 60 and 120 days ago. The score is the average of the three signs, from −1 to +1.
  2. Divide by the coin's recent volatility, so riskier coins get smaller positions.
  3. Scale the whole portfolio so its expected volatility is 20% a year.
  4. Apply caps: at most 25% per coin and at most 2× gross leverage. Rebalance once a day at 00:00 UTC.

Why: every setting comes from published research or a round-number default, not from searching for the best backtest. That keeps the number of free parameters, and therefore the room to overfit, very small.

5.2 Funding carry (diversifier)

Buy a coin on spot and short the same amount on perpetuals. Price moves cancel out, and the position collects funding when leveraged longs pay shorts. The rebuilt version holds at most 5 coins with fixed weights and rebalances daily. It only enters if 14 days of expected funding cover the round-trip trading cost at least twice, and it skips coins whose spot and perp prices diverge by more than 2%.

Why it was rebuilt: the first version rebalanced every 8 hours and spent more on trading costs than it earned in funding. Funding has also become much lower since 2024, so carry now trades rarely. That is by design.

6Execution and cost model

  • No look-ahead: a decision made with the close of hour t is filled at the open of hour t+1. Automated tests check that cutting off future data never changes a past decision.
  • Costs on every trade: exchange fee (spot 10 bps, perp 5 bps), half the bid-ask spread, and market impact (square-root law: impact grows with trade size relative to daily volume).
  • Funding: charged or received at every funding time on positions actually held.
  • Delistings: forced exit at twice the normal cost.
  • Stress test: every result is re-run at 1.5× and 2× costs.
  • Reconciliation: an independent module recomputes every fee, spread and impact charge and checks that equity changes equal market P&L minus costs.

10Live paper-trading system

Run once an hour with engine live step. No real orders are ever sent.

  1. Fetch the latest closed hourly bars and funding from Binance's public API and merge them with stored history. The bar still in progress is always dropped.
  2. Fill the previous hour's orders at this hour's open, with modelled costs.
  3. Accrue funding and mark equity to market.
  4. Risk check, then decide new target weights with the same code used in backtests.
  5. Log to an append-only signal record with UTC timestamps. That record is the public track record.
  6. Publish a Telegram message with weights, changes, risk notes and a mandatory disclaimer. It is sent only when a bot token is configured and --send is passed.

12Risks and limitations (full disclosure)

  • Edge not yet statistically proven. On unseen data the Sharpe confidence interval includes zero. More live data is needed.
  • Overfitting warning. PBO for trend is 0.94 on the development period: among the 9 trend variants tried, the in-sample winner tended not to win out of sample. Our response is to use published default settings and never pick the best variant.
  • The holdout has been viewed 5 times, mostly for bug fixes with no tuning, and every view is logged. Repeated viewing slowly erodes how clean the test is. From now on, only live paper results count as new evidence.
  • Expected live performance is lower. Research suggests live Sharpe is often about half the backtest: realistically 0.5–0.9, drawdowns of 15–25%, and flat years.
  • Market structure risk. Exchange failure (FTX-style), regulatory changes, liquidity crises and funding regime shifts.
  • Small universe. 16 coins. The point-in-time universe expansion is in progress.
  • Legal. Paid signals may count as investment advice in many countries, including India. Legal review is needed before charging. No profit promises, ever.

13Roadmap

  1. Now: freeze the code (first version-control commit) and start the hourly public paper track record.
  2. Months 1–3: free Telegram channel publishing every signal and every loss; monthly performance reports.
  3. In parallel: point-in-time universe of the top coins by volume; long-only spot variant; measured live slippage vs model.
  4. Month 3–6: if live results stay within the expected range, review legal requirements and consider a paid tier.
  5. Later: real execution only with small capital, reconciliation against actual fills, and independent audit of the track record.

14Glossary

Sharpe ratioAnnual return divided by annual volatility. Above 1 is good for a single strategy; BTC's long-run value is about 0.5–1.
Max drawdownThe largest peak-to-trough fall in account value.
CAGRCompound annual growth rate.
Perpetual future (perp)A futures contract with no expiry; it allows shorting and leverage, and pays or receives funding every few hours.
Funding rateThe periodic payment between perp longs and shorts that keeps the perp price close to spot.
HoldoutData kept locked during development and used only for a final test.
OverfittingTuning a model to past noise. It looks great on history and fails live.
bpsBasis points: 1 bp = 0.01%.
